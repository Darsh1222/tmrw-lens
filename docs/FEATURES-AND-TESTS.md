# Tomorrow Lens: features, usefulness and test plan

Prepared November 17, 2026. **These are expected test results, not a claim that students have completed them.** The product has 345 passing unit tests in the latest checked run; live provider access, external assistant delivery and real student outcomes need separate verification.

## The strongest case to present

Lens connects three useful steps: inspect the evidence behind a deadline, find a realistic next action, and recover unfinished work without restarting completed progress. A short demonstration can prove that connection. No win probability or world-first claim is justified by the evidence available.

## Five tests to prioritize before recording

1. **Check a real source.** Add the supplied synthetic course outline with the live reader, inspect its worksheet date and exact quote, then add the contradictory update. Confirm which date should be used. Save the evidence panel and the result. If the live reader is unavailable, label the sample demonstration recorded and treat fresh reading as an unresolved submission check.
2. **A meaningful time restriction.** In the frozen recovery example type “I'm stuck and only have 20 minutes tonight.” Preview. Confirm today has no more than 20 minutes and the full 60 minutes is still scheduled.
3. **A concrete interruption.** Reset the example. Type “I'm busy until 8 PM.” Preview. Confirm the unavailable interval is visible and the task starts at or after 8 PM.
4. **Progress survives.** Reset to the default stuck example without the 20-minute cap. Apply its 10-minute starter plus 50-minute remainder. Finish the first step. Choose Plans changed and a free-again time later than the next session's start. Preview and approve. Confirm the completed 10 never returns to the unfinished amount.
5. **Honest failure.** Give an isolated test task less available time than the remaining work needs. It should say a complete plan does not fit and preserve the current task, rather than drop work or invent hours.

Save a screenshot or short recording per scenario, plus the input and the expected/actual result. Run the same sequence on the deployed site, not only localhost.

## A short student test

Invite 3–5 students to try the same synthetic material voluntarily. Say: “We are testing the app, not you. Please tell me what you expect to happen.” Do not coach the first attempt.

Ask each person to: (1) identify what Lens does from the opening page, (2) find the evidence for a deadline, (3) plan one task, (4) recover after finishing a starter, and (5) explain how much work is left. Record whether they complete each task without help, the time taken, errors/corrections and their explanation of the next action.

For an exploratory comparison, have them manually plan the same amount of work too, using equivalent tasks and alternating which method comes first. Report individual times, sample size and failures; practice effects and convenience sampling limit any conclusion. Ask “What was confusing?” and “Would this help after an interruption? Why?”

Success evidence is an observed task completed correctly or a measured reduction in planning/correction effort for that small sample. Do not convert a few preferences into a claim about grades, mental health or all students. The student-results CSV is empty until you conduct the tests.

## What to say accurately

- “Lens links extracted deadlines to source evidence and asks when information is unclear.”
- “The local planner checks supplied availability and accounts for the full remaining task.”
- “Completing a step preserves reported progress during another recovery.”
- “The AI reads course material; code performs date and constraint checks. Typed recovery notes use a bounded local phrase reader.”
- “Current evaluation evidence is synthetic and recorded. We are testing usefulness with students.” Only change that last sentence after collecting real results.

Do not claim automatic external-calendar synchronization, a full semester's study-task optimization, general-purpose chat understanding, a clinically validated stress benefit, improved grades, a statistically proven time saving, or a guaranteed hackathon win. The main workspace keeps multiple saved tasks with one focused task card. The separate assistant requires its own integration.
